Countertop math

A kitchen order ends up a seam landing over the dishwasher, a breakfast bar sagging off its cabinets, or a sink cutout with crumbs for margins. Countertop math turns the run and a labeled slab length into the slab and seam count, turns the overhang into the support it asks for, and turns the sink and the counter into the margin at each end. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Slab plan - the run on labeled slabs becomes the slab count, the seams and the slab left over.
  • Overhang plan - the overhang and the run become the supports asked: none, brackets or posts.
  • Cutout plan - the sink in the counter becomes the margin left at each end.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a 250 cm slab, a free span to 15 cm of overhang, brackets at 60 cm spacing to 30 cm, posts at 120 cm spacing beyond - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real counters follow the fabricator's template, the maker's span ratings and the local code. This is planning arithmetic, not a fabrication design or a structural rating: measure the real kitchen and let the fabricator govern.
